Elias Thorne — The Midnight Oracle
Age: 24 Race: Human Class: Warlock — Level 9 Alignment: Neutral Role: Strategist, Prophet & Keeper of Secrets
Elias is the youngest and most mysterious of the three. He rarely speaks unless he has something important to say.
He doesn't command people directly. Instead, he gives them pieces of information and allows them to reach the conclusions he wants.
Some members believe Elias possesses supernatural knowledge.
Others suspect his knowledge comes from somewhere much darker.
•     Personality: Quiet, intelligent, mysterious, unnervingly calm.
•     Strength: Planning several moves ahead.
•     Weakness: His obsession with forbidden knowledge.
•     Leadership style: Controls information rather than people.
•     D&D abilities: Pact of the Tome, with spells such as Detect Thoughts, Invisibility, Clairvoyance, and Misty Visions.
•     Secret: His patron showed him a vision of the future where Elias himself destroys the secret society.
Growing up:
Elias Thorne — The Midnight Oracle
Elias had the strangest childhood of the three.
He was born in a poor district of a major city and raised by his mother, a quiet scholar who worked as a translator and archivist.
They had very little money, but their home was filled with books.
Elias learned to read before he was five.
By seven, he was asking questions his mother couldn't answer.
By nine, he was reading books intended for adults.
By eleven, he had developed a fascination with forbidden subjects: ancient civilizations, forgotten gods, magical artifacts, secret organizations, and prophecies.
His mother encouraged his curiosity—but she had one rule:
"Some knowledge is dangerous because of what it tells you. Other knowledge is dangerous because of what it makes you become."
Elias didn't understand the difference.
At fourteen, he discovered a hidden collection of forbidden texts inside the archive where his mother worked.
One particular book fascinated him.
It contained fragments of prophecies written centuries earlier.
Most were nonsense.
One wasn't.
It described events that hadn't happened yet.
Elias began secretly investigating the prophecy.
For two years, he became obsessed.
He eventually discovered that several events from the prophecy had already occurred exactly as described.
Then he found something that terrified him.
The final pages described a young man who would create—or inherit—a secret organization capable of changing the world.
The man's name was never given.
But beside the prophecy was a symbol.
The same symbol Elias had seen repeatedly in his dreams.
At seventeen, Elias began having visions.
At first they were small: a broken glass, a stranger entering a room, a fire in a distant building.
Then the visions became more significant.
He started predicting events before they happened.
His mother became frightened.
She begged him to stop searching.
Elias refused.
When he was nineteen, his mother disappeared.
No body was ever found.
The only thing left behind was a note containing four words:
"Stop looking for me."
Elias never did.
Instead, he became obsessed with discovering what happened to her.
His investigation eventually led him toward the secret society.
And eventually, toward something even more disturbing:
His mother's disappearance may have been connected to the society itself.
That is why Elias doesn't simply want to lead the organization.
He wants to uncover the truth behind it.
Even if that truth destroys him.
