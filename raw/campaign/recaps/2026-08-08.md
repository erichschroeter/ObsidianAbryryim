## Bea’s journal
Leaves from Bea’s Journal

12th of Flamerule

My old knees demand the support of an oak cane these days, but my hands remain steady enough to glaze a tray of honey cakes before dawn. Maren at The Golden Crust took the entire batch today with a warm smile, totally unaware that her bustling counter is about to become the epicenter of our new Jiong-Tis network.

28th of Eleasis

I watched Cora hand a handful of her hard-earned coins to a young couple crying on their doorstep so they could make rent—a gesture so quiet and selfless she thought no one saw. I caught up to her at the tavern after her shift and bought us a bottle of dry red; behind those tired, early-morning eyes lies a soul as good and sharp as any I’ve met.

14th of Marpenoth

Saw Guard Captain Elara sneaking bread to the street urchins in the alley and made sure to hand out hot honey cakes to the whole crowd. On our walk today, Durgash asked why some people have so little while others have so much; I gave him no easy answers, only the truth, and watched the gears turn in his young mind. He is going to grow into a fine Orc, and I treasure our conversations more than he knows.
3rd of Uktar

Struck up a conversation with a family of weary travelers near the gate and directed them straight to Tobias at The Hearth & Lantern. Ever since I saw him open his doors to the homeless during last week's freeze, I’ve made it my personal mission to keep his rooms full and his heart supported.

11th of Uktar

Owen stopped by after a long day of tedious court politics, looking completely weighed down by his new mantle of nobility. I chopped vegetables for a stew and simply listened, tossing out a quiet question here and there; by the time the soup was cooked, he had talked his way through his own doubts and found his answers, leaving with lighter shoulders and a full belly.

17th of Nightal

Durgash fell asleep halfway through the story of how T-Man held the line at the bridge, his little hands curled tightly in my sleeve. I stayed sitting beside him in the quiet candlelight long after his breathing slowed, wondering if his father knows just how much of his own courage lives in this boy's heart.

15th of Hammer

Min came over late after a grueling shift at the temple, still smelling of incense and rain. We sat by the hearth polishing the links of her chainmail over hot herbal tea, a quiet ritual we've come to treasure; I will forever be grateful to her for delivering that letter about the Liars Syndicate, but seeing her finally relax her shoulders is the true blessing.

19th of Hammer

Sat out on the stoop at sunset with my new custom lute, plucking through a clumsy rendition of an old ballad while Durgash, Min, and Sir Owen politely pretended I sounded magnificent. All three took turns singing to cover my missed notes; my fingers are stiff, but the laughter we shared as the sun dipped below the horizon was sweet enough to cure any ache.

8th of Alturiak

Min joined Sir Owen and me for a fine glass of wine today, and for an hour we simply talked about nothing at all while the chill of evening set in. I enjoyed the conversation and the company.
24th of Ches

Durgash asked today if his father would be proud of how fast he can run now. I leaned heavily on my cane, looked him square in the eyes, and told him his father was proud of him long before he could even walk. The beam on that young boy's face warmed and he skipped along the rest of the walk.

29th of Ches

Durgash sat across from me at the kitchen table today, his little hands barely big enough to fan out a full hand of Three-Dragon Ante cards without dropping them. He tried so hard to keep a straight face when he drew the Gold Monach, but those green ears of his twitched with excitement anyway; he's got a sharp eye for the game, and watching him learn reminds me that every grand legacy starts with the simplest of lessons.

2nd of Mirtul

Reliving my adventures with Lyza in my mind, I am resolved: her shyness is not weakness, but a cloak that lets her true strength hide in plain sight. Choosing her mother’s victims over her mother’s favor proved her character, and she is the one who will quietly carry the Jiong-Tis far beyond this town. My letter to her rests safely in my cloak pocket; she is the perfect successor, and I pray I am here to hand it to her myself.

4th of Mirtul

We built a fire in the yard tonight as the twilight turned warm, sharing a fine bottle of red while Owen, Min, and I took turns telling Durgash stories about T-Man until the stars began to fade. The boy sat spellbound, his eyes reflecting the embers, soaking in every legend of his father’s bravery until he finally drifted off right there on the grass.

18th of Kythorn

On our walk today, Durgash helped me carry a basket containing a dark wine for Owen, Min, and me, along with a tin of sweet chamomile tea I knew Min would adore. Out of nowhere, the boy looked up and asked if being brave means you never feel scared; I told him true bravery only exists when you are afraid, and watched him nod with a wisdom far beyond his years.
18th of Mirtul

I’ve spent the last few afternoons humming and scratching out music notes on scrap parchment—a new song, built just for when the old crew finally walks back through that door. My fingers aren't as fast as they used to be and the tune is simple, but it’s packed with every story we ever shared. I’ve tucked the parchment safely inside the secret compartment of my lute for safekeeping. I can’t wait to sit on the stoop, look them in the eye, and show them this old woman still has a few surprises left up her sleeve.

## Letter to Vara
Vara,

We finally made it back from the Feywild, though apparently being gone for a year has made coming home rather complicated.

We arrived in Brixton’s Crossing with a prince, a pontiff, and thirty-eight guards. Naturally, there was a festival planned to celebrate our return. I was hoping for a quiet drink.

Instead, I met a local smith, who knows considerably more about smithing than I do and has been teaching me a thing or two.

I've been gifted a piece of Dark Diamond ore. Unfortunately, the local forge couldn't get hot enough to work it. Luckily, I've somehow ended up with the deed to a dwarven city near Pistivo that supposedly has a forge capable of shaping it.

Then things became less pleasant.

A companion I had been traveling with, Bea, died the night before the festival.
And then, because apparently our return wasn't dramatic enough, a **three-houses-tall red dragon** demolished part of the town.

So much for a quiet homecoming.

I hope wherever you are, you're staying out of trouble.

Though knowing you, that's probably asking too much.

—Brokkur
