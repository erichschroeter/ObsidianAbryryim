### Remlin Notes
After several days in monastic reverie, Remlin emerges with a Scroll of Fly in hand to the news of the great Bard Harles Alnwick's demise.  He quickly tracks down the party to share the following letter that he received from his mother and urges the party to help him find a way to briefly visit the Feywild:

My Dearest Son,
If this letter has found you before the butterflies have learned to swim, then the post has finally remembered which direction tomorrow lives. If not, do not scold the envelope. It has always been terribly poor at telling left from yesterday.  I hope your shadow still follows you willingly; mine has grown rather independent of late and insists upon drinking tea with mushrooms who refuse to exist unless no one is looking at them. It has become quite exhausting.

I write because the Feywild has misplaced something dreadfully important, but no one remembers exactly what it is.  The rivers now flow in circles to avoid arriving; trees have begun whispering secrets that they have not yet heard; the moon rises twice on Thursdays but refuses to appear at all when invited politely. Even the flowers have started blooming into questions instead of blossoms.  Naturally, most everyone insists that this is perfectly ordinary; you and I know that it is not.

The Court of Laughing Leaves has held thirteen emergency celebrations about the matter, though each feast concludes before it begins. The Duchess of Dandelions accused Time of stealing itself, but Time simply bowed, apologized for next week, and wandered off wearing someone else's face.  Only one name continues to appear where memories should be.  Yours, Remlin...
Do you remember, little Acorn, when you were scarcely taller than a fox's grin? The crickets fell silent whenever you laughed, as though listening for a melody older than stars. Your grandmother told me then that one day the forest itself would ask a favor of you.  I thought it was another of her delightful impossibilities, but now I am no longer certain.
There is, somewhere beyond the Hollow That Cannot Decide Whether It Is Inside or Out, a crooked path woven from forgotten promises. Follow it only when it refuses to stay beneath your feet. If it behaves sensibly, you have found the wrong road.  When you come upon a table laid for guests who have not yet arrived, sit in the empty chair that looks occupied and accept no tea unless the teacup objects. The quietest riddle is always the loudest answer.
Should a hare ask your name, tell him your favorite memory instead. If a caterpillar offers directions, politely disagree before thanking him, and if you meet a queen wearing a crown of thorns and daisies, curtsy exactly once, smile exactly twice, and never admit that roses know how to bleed.  Most importantly bring your kindness I know is within you.  Iron may wound a fey, fire may frighten one, but kindness rearranges the world in ways even magic struggles to predict.
I
wish I could tell you more, but every sentence I write after this insists upon becoming a different sentence by morning. The ink has become mischievous, and I suspect it is secretly employed by riddles.  Know this, my beloved Remlin: not all who wander in the Feywild are lost, some are simply being borrowed by wonder.
If you hear someone singing your cradle song where no singer stands, follow it. That will be me, waiting where the impossible finally makes sense.  Please come soon; the seasons have begun forgetting your name, and I fear they may forget mine next.
With every leaf that has ever danced, with every moonbeam that ever learned to laugh, your loving Mother,
 Lethariel, of the Silver Briars, Ast’eria
