## Letter to Vara

Vara,

You’ll be pleased (or perhaps mildly horrified) to know that your favorite overconfident engineer is still in one piece—mostly. I recently found myself in a rather lively disagreement with an ogre. Not the charming, misunderstood kind either. This one had _blades embedded in his arms_. Yes, arms plural. As in, two of them. As in, twice the poor decision-making for me to stand in front of.

Naturally, I did the sensible thing and stood directly in his way.

You’d have laughed to see it—me, all proud in my armor, thinking, “Ah yes, Brokkur, today you are indestructible.” Two swings later—_two!_—and I was left humbled. I’m fairly certain I briefly saw my ancestors shaking their heads at me.

But! I held. I kept that lumbering slab of muscle and bad ideas focused on me long enough for the others to do their work. Turns out, getting repeatedly clobbered is a surprisingly effective strategy when done with confidence and a complete disregard for personal safety.

I couldn’t help but think of you out there on the edge of those freezing waters—facing something vast, dark, and far too dangerous for anyone to handle alone. It’s a strange thing, standing your ground when every instinct says to run. But you do it. You always have. I suppose I was trying, in my own foolish way, to live up to that kind of courage.

Don’t worry—I’ve already started sketching improvements. If I can make something that lets you defy the crushing depths, I can certainly make something that stops an ogre from turning me into a dented kettle.

Stay safe out there, Vara. And… if you ever find yourself thinking the dark is closing in, just remember: somewhere, I’m probably being hit by something much larger, and still standing.

Yours (remarkably unflattened),  
Brokkur
